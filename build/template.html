Data & caveats

Results: unofficial November 3, 2026 returns from each state's own election office. Nothing is certified yet; counts will keep moving. Where a state was available from several collectors, the newest official feed won. Every race shown and its source:

Still gray, and why: no county-level results reachable yet for New York, Illinois, Michigan (2 counties only), Wisconsin (Milwaukee only), New Jersey (4 counties only), Kentucky, Mississippi, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Wyoming, Kansas, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire or Nevada: their sites either block automated access, publish only after certification, or (Michigan, New Jersey) leave results to each county clerk. Alaska reports by state House district, not borough. Other gray states had no Senate or governor race. Wikipedia's county tables for 2026 aren't up yet either.

Two-way margin: each race uses its top two candidates statewide, whatever their party, so Nebraska, Montana, South Dakota and Idaho's Senate races (Republican vs independent) and Rhode Island's governor race (Democrat vs independent) are drawn honestly. Gold = an independent leads the county.

Height: population density = 2024 Census population estimate ÷ land area (sq mi, 2024 Gazetteer). Connecticut uses 2020 estimates for its old counties. "Votes" uses total votes cast in the selected race.

Color: (Republican − Democratic) ÷ all votes in the race, saturating at 40 points. Independents/minor parties count toward the total only.

Shapes: US Census county boundaries via us-atlas, Albers USA projection (Alaska/Hawaii inset).

Built Nov 12, 2026. Statewide sums were checked against the sources' own statewide totals, and several races (TX, SC Senate) against AP calls in news reports.
